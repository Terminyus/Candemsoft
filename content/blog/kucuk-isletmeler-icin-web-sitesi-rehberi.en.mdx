## Common pitfalls small businesses face

Small businesses frequently operate under tight budgets and deadlines, creating fertile ground for agency traps:

- **Vendor lock-in:** When an agency registers your domain or hosting under their own account, you lose control. If you switch providers, transferring assets can trigger costly disputes.
- **Bloated templates:** Themes packed with dozens of plugins and page builders look complete, but they crawl on mobile devices and drive away visitors.
- **Hidden fees:** Low upfront estimates often conceal expensive charges for simple text edits, security patches, or domain renewals.

Clear technical agreements before kickoff prevent these problems.

## 7 essential questions to ask your web agency

### 1. Who owns the domain registration and hosting accounts?

Your domain name is your primary digital property. It must be registered under your business name using your corporate email on an independent registrar. The agency should only receive technical management access. The same rule applies to web hosting. If invoices are not billed to your company and you lack administrative access, an agency dispute could take your site offline.

### 2. Who owns the source code and intellectual property?

Will you receive the full source code upon delivery, or is the site hosted on a closed proprietary system? In a professional engagement, custom code, styles, and database schemas developed for you must belong to your company. Code should reside in a modern Git repository and be transferred to your organization at launch. Without repository access, switching developers in the future may require rebuilding everything from scratch.

### 3. What are the mobile responsiveness and Google Lighthouse benchmarks?

Most web traffic comes from mobile devices. Google prioritizes mobile-first indexing and uses Core Web Vitals metrics to rank results. Ask what Lighthouse scores the agency commits to achieving. Responsive design demands modern image formats (WebP or AVIF), minified JavaScript, minimal render-blocking CSS, and rapid First Contentful Paint.

### 4. How will technical SEO and structured data be handled?

Publishing a website online does not guarantee search discovery. Technical SEO must be incorporated during development rather than patched on later. Ask how the agency implements:

- Unique page titles and meta descriptions for every route,
- Semantic HTML tags respecting heading hierarchies,
- Structured schema markup (Schema.org / JSON-LD) for business location and contact details,
- Clean, automatically generated sitemap.xml and robots.txt files,
- Open Graph tags ensuring social preview cards render accurately.

### 5. How easy is it for internal staff to update content?

A business website is an active asset. Updating operating hours or publishing announcements should not require emailing a programmer every time. Request an intuitive Content Management System (CMS) without dashboard clutter, and verify whether hands-on training is included in the project scope.

### 6. How are SSL certificates and data security handled?

HTTPS encryption with an active SSL certificate is mandatory for visitor trust, data protection, and search rankings. Contact forms must be protected against automated spam bots using modern defenses like Turnstile. Customer submission data must be transferred securely and comply with privacy regulations.

### 7. What are the post-launch maintenance and support terms?

A website lifecycle continues after launch. Server operating systems need patches, dependencies update, and outages require fast resolution. Ask what warranty period is provided, what the guaranteed response time (SLA) for bugs is, and whether automated backups are scheduled regularly.

## Contractual details to secure before project kickoff

When entering an agreement, ensure the formal proposal replaces vague promises with concrete technical requirements:

- Full ownership and delivery of uncompiled source code via a dedicated Git repository,
- Direct administrative credentials for domain, DNS, and hosting dashboards,
- Defined minimum mobile Lighthouse performance benchmarks,
- A minimum 30-day post-launch warranty covering bug fixes at no extra charge.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does a small business website cost?**
Website pricing depends on scope and complexity. Reconfiguring a basic theme involves fundamentally different effort than building custom software with bespoke admin panels. Page count, multilingual requirements, and integrations all influence the budget. Instead of fixed generic packages, request a transparent proposal tailored to your actual specifications.

**Should small businesses choose pre-made templates or custom development?**
This choice depends on budget and growth trajectory. For temporary experiments, quality templates provide a fast start. However, when unique branding, peak loading speeds, strong security, and tailored workflows are necessary, custom code provides greater reliability and longevity.

**Can we switch to another developer after the website is delivered?**
Yes, provided your site is built using standard technologies, the Git repository is handed over, and full intellectual property rights are assigned in your contract. If your site is hosted on a closed proprietary system without code export, you will remain reliant on the original agency.

**How long does it take to build a corporate website?**
Timelines vary based on feature complexity and content readiness. A straightforward informational site can typically be delivered within a few weeks, whereas complex custom integrations take longer. Providing your approved branding assets and text copy promptly is the key factor in staying on schedule.
